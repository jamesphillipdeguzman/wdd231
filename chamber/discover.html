Iloilo Overview

  • Population: ~2.5 million (Province & City)
  • Languages: Hiligaynon, Kinaray-a, English, Filipino
  • Literacy Rate: ~95%
  • Key Drivers: Agriculture, Trade, IT-BPO, Tourism, Manufacturing
  • Accolade: UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy
See more facts

Regional Hub: Serves as the administrative and financial capital of Western Visayas (Region VI).

Culture & Heritage: Renowned for the Dinagyang Festival, well-preserved heritage mansions along Calle Real, and a vibrant arts scene.

Infrastructure: Home to the Iloilo International Airport, Iloilo River Esplanade, and modern commercial hubs like Festive Walk and Iloilo Business Park.

Santa Barbara Parish Church

A historical Baroque-Renaissance church built in 1845, which served as the headquarters of the revolutionary forces under General Martin Delgado.

See more

Location: Santa Barbara, Iloilo (approx. 15 km north of Iloilo City).

Historical Significance: Site of the historic "Cry of Santa Barbara" on November 17, 1898, where the Philippine flag was first raised outside Luzon.

Architecture: Constructed from yellow sandstone and limestone, features a grand convent-museum preserved with Spanish-era artifacts.

Guimaras Strait & Eco-Tourism

A pristine island province located just across the Iloilo Strait, globally celebrated for exporting the sweetest mangoes and tranquil eco-beaches.

See more

Access: 15-minute pumpboat or RORO ferry ride from Parola Wharf in Iloilo City.

Key Highlights: Guisi Lighthouse (18th-century Spanish ruins), Alubihod Beach, marine reserves, Trappist Monastic Food Products, and wind turbine eco-farms.

Eco-Tourism: Ideal for day tours, island-hopping, snorkeling, and cycling excursions along scenic coastal trails. Best season is the Manggahan Festival held annually every May.

Festive Walk Iloilo

The centerpiece lifestyle, dining, and retail promenade located within the Iloilo Business Park in Mandurriao.

See more

Location: Mandurriao, Iloilo City.

Attractions & Highlights: Features the Iloilo Museum of Contemporary Art (ILOMOCA), pet-friendly outdoor dining strips, craft breweries, and open-air public art installations.

Nightlife & Events: Hub for evening entertainment, weekend artisan fairs, and culinary events reflecting Iloilo's UNESCO Gastronomy status.

Iloilo River Esplanade

An acclaimed 9-kilometer linear river park designed by architect Paulo Alcazaren, revitalizing the historic Iloilo River into an urban recreation greenway.

See more

Activities: Popular for sunset jogs, cycling, bird watching, and outdoor waterfront dining across multiple connected sections.

Recognition: Recipient of national urban design awards for sustainable river rehabilitation, resilience, and public open spaces.

Miag-ao Church (Santo Tomás de Villanueva)

A UNESCO World Heritage Baroque church built in 1797 with native yellow-ochre limestone, renowned for its intricate botanical façade reliefs.

See more

Location: Miag-ao, Southern Iloilo (approx. 40 km west of Iloilo City).

Façade Artistry: Features Saint Christopher carrying the Christ Child flanked by native coconut and papaya trees sculpted in limestone.

Fortress Design: Built with massive 1.5-meter-thick stone walls and twin watchtowers to protect against coastal pirate raids.

Molo Church (The Feminist Church)

A Neo-Gothic landmark constructed in 1831, featuring 16 statues of female saints flanking its central nave.

See more

Location: Molo Plaza, Iloilo City.

Cultural Note: Jose Rizal visited this church in 1898, noting its distinctive red spires. Located across the plaza from the historic Molo Mansion.

Local Specialty: Walk across to sample authentic Pancit Molo right by the plaza.

Molo Mansion (Yusay-Consing)

A preserved 1920s heritage mansion showcasing classic Philippine colonial architecture, cultural exhibitions, and artisanal local crafts.

See more

Location: Locsin Street, Molo, Iloilo City.

Significance: Rescued and fully restored as a cultural center featuring high ceilings, hardwood arches, and traditional capiz window panes.

Visitor Tips: Stroll the manicured garden café, purchase authentic Ilonggo woven textiles (hablon), and enjoy native tablea hot chocolate.

Museum of Philippine Economic History

Housed inside the historic Ynchausti y Compañía building, chronicling the rich trade routes, sugar boom, and mercantile legacy of Iloilo.

See more

Location: Ortiz Street, Calle Real Heritage Zone, City Proper, Iloilo City.

Significance: The first museum in the Philippines exclusively dedicated to economic history, spanning pre-colonial trading to the 19th-century sugar boom.

Visitor Tips: Admission is free. Interactive multimedia dioramas and century-old artifacts depict the industrialization of Panay.
